Bangladesh's 14-day business startup plan — from 355 days to a single ID

October 2026 · 3 min read

The news: The commerce ministry and Invest Bangladesh are jointly developing a 14-day Business Readiness Package under BanglaBiz, the single-window platform for business and investment services, aiming to cut Bangladesh's business-startup timeline from an average of 355 days down to 14. Entrepreneurs will receive a unique Business ID through which they can apply for licences, clearances, and approvals — trade licence, TIN, bank account, fire and environmental approvals, and more — without resubmitting the same documents to each agency separately. A dashboard will track which approvals are obtained, pending, or stalled. Invest Bangladesh has already mapped the steps required to start a business, work has begun on amending relevant regulations, and officials expect some initial results within a month, with the full system operational within roughly a year. Separately, some voices in the business community — including BUILD Chairperson Abul Kasem Khan — have argued the reform should go further, replacing the trade licence system itself with a single Business Identification Number usable for banking, mobile financial services, and trade.

Source: The Business Standard — read the full report

A fantastic initiative, if it lands

This is a fantastic initiative by the government. In Bangladesh, where starting and operating a business can often feel like a nightmare due to regulatory complexity, the expansion of BanglaBiz can open a new door for entrepreneurs.

Fewer in-person touchpoints means fewer opportunities for the friction that slows entrepreneurs down.

Transparency is the underrated part

Digitising and integrating more services into a single platform can significantly reduce paperwork, prerequisites and the time required to start a business. It could also reduce opportunities for bribery by minimising unnecessary in-person interactions. The application-tracking dashboard will further improve transparency and give entrepreneurs greater confidence by allowing them to monitor the status of their applications.

BanglaBiz must succeed in this journey — not just as a digital platform, but as a genuine reform that makes doing business easier for the people of Bangladesh.
