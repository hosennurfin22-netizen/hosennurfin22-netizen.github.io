DP World takes on the New Mooring Container Terminal — a big deal that must be measured

October 2026 · 4 min read

The news: The Chittagong Port Authority and DP World signed a 15-year concession on 8 October for DP World to operate, maintain and modernise the New Mooring Container Terminal (NCT) and its overflow yard, while CPA keeps ownership of the land, infrastructure and regulatory control. DP World will pay roughly Tk600 crore upfront and commit more than Tk1,000 crore of investment over the first decade, with the government citing a broader package of around $150 million. CPA will receive 67% of terminal revenue per container handled plus a fixed annual fee of about Tk10 crore, and the deal carries a minimum annual volume guarantee of 1.23 million TEUs. The terminal handled roughly 44% of Chattogram Port's containers last fiscal year, and currently ranks 364th in the Container Port Performance Index 2025, with containers sitting an average of 9.4 days. Officials expect DP World to begin work within six months and reach full operations within two years — though the full agreement, including performance benchmarks and revenue-sharing terms, has not been made public, and workers' representatives have raised concerns over job security and transparency.

Source: The Business Standard — read the full report

Why this is a major step

The government deserves appreciation for finalising this significant agreement with DP World. Bringing a globally experienced port operator to modernise and operate the New Mooring Container Terminal is a major step toward improving Bangladesh's port efficiency and logistics capabilities.

Persistent operational inefficiencies, technological gaps and capacity constraints have long challenged our port operations. DP World's expertise, investment and technology can help address these gaps, reduce cargo-handling time and strengthen Bangladesh's competitiveness in global trade.

The deal is the easy part. Delivery is where it gets measured.

Where the real test begins

This is a big deal for Bangladesh. The real success, however, will depend on transparent implementation, measurable performance improvements and effective regulatory oversight to ensure long-term benefits for the country.
